AC Sizing Calculator Checklist: What to Check Before You Sign a Quote

Square footage alone is a poor guide to air conditioner size. Work through these checks before you accept a tonnage on a quote, and you will know whether the number holds up.

Do you know the floor area and ceiling height?

Measure the conditioned floor area, not the lot or the garage. The common starting point is about 20 BTU/h per square foot for a house with 8-foot ceilings; taller ceilings scale the load up in proportion. One ton of cooling is 12,000 BTU/h.

Have you allowed for climate?

Design temperatures in Phoenix and Minneapolis are more than 20 °F apart, and humid climates add a large moisture (latent) load. The same house can need a noticeably different size in a hot-humid, hot-dry, mixed, cool or cold climate.

How good are the insulation and air sealing?

An older, leaky house can need 25 to 40% more capacity than a tight new one of the same size. Compare your attic and wall insulation with the recommended R-values for your climate zone before trusting a rule of thumb.

Have the windows been counted separately?

Glass gains far more heat per square foot than an insulated wall. In the reference house about a quarter of summer heat gain comes through windows. Window area, glazing type (single pane, clear double, low-E, triple) and orientation all change that share, and unshaded west-facing glass is the largest single heat source on a summer afternoon.

Where are the ducts?

Attics reach 130 to 150 °F. Leaky or poorly insulated ducts up there can lose 15 to 30% of the cooling before it reaches a room, which is why duct location belongs in the estimate.

People and cooking

Each regular occupant beyond the first two adds about 600 BTU/h. An open-plan kitchen adds more heat to the living space than a closed one, at the time of day the outdoor load is still high.

Was the size rounded sensibly?

Round to the nearest available size rather than always rounding up. ACCA Manual S generally allows single-stage cooling capacity up to 115% of the calculated load. Going well past that brings short cycling and a cold, clammy house. If you are between sizes, a two-stage or variable-speed unit is usually a better buy than the next size up.

Is there a Manual J behind the quote?

A quick estimate checks a quote; it does not replace a room-by-room ACCA Manual J load calculation, which most codes require for new installations. Run your numbers through the AC tonnage calculator, and if the quote differs a lot, ask to see the load calculation inputs. For a single room, window or portable unit, use the room BTU calculator instead.
